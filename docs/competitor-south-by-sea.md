# Competitor brief: South by Sea

_Research date: October 2026. Sources at the bottom. Their own website blocked automated reading, so details come from their FAQ and job listings as indexed by search engines. Check them yourself before quoting any of this to a customer._

## Who they are
- Custom apparel company based in Philadelphia, founded around 2011.
- **Built around Greek life.** Their site is titled "Custom Sorority and Fraternity Apparel," and they are a **licensed Greek merch provider**.
- They sell through a **campus manager** network: college students working 5–10 hrs/week for **8–12% commission**. Reps prospect orgs, pitch designs, and pass orders to a sales manager.
- They also have a self-serve **Design Studio** and run **"Live Sales"**.

## How their ordering works
| | South by Sea |
|---|---|
| Getting a price | Fill out a "Get Started" form, then get a **mockup + quote within 24 business hours** |
| Minimum | **10 pieces per printed design** recommended |
| Group ordering | Online buy link. The coordinator picks a minimum that sets pricing. Cards are charged at purchase and **refunded if the minimum isn't met** |
| Deadline | Order must **close 10 full business days before** you need it |
| Turnaround | **9–12 business days** standard, plus 2–5 days if shipped individually |
| Pricing | Depends on product, decoration, quantity and licensing. They **price-match** licensed competitors' formal quotes |
| Track record | BBB lists 16 complaints closed in the last 3 years |

## Where they are strong (don't fight them here)
1. **Greek life.** They hold the licenses, the brand recognition and the chapter relationships.
2. **Scale and catalog.** Huge product range, a design team, and production capacity.
3. **A rep on almost every campus**, and Fresh Prints works the same way.

## Where you win
| Their weak spot | Your edge | Where it shows on the site |
|---|---|---|
| Greek-first focus, so everyone else is an afterthought | **Every club that isn't Greek:** club sports, intramurals, business and academic clubs, dorms, service orgs | Hero and first "Why us" card |
| Price only after a form and a wait | **Instant price calculator** on the homepage | Pricing section |
| ~10 piece minimum | **1-piece minimum** (digital print), automatic screen-print discount at 12+ | "Order from 1 piece" card, FAQ |
| Rep on commission passes you to a sales manager | **The founder handles you start to finish** and you can text him | "One person" card |
| Shipped from Philadelphia, often in individual boxes | **Hand-delivered on campus, sorted by name** | How it works, step 4 |
| Fundraising isn't the pitch | **Fundraiser calculator + stores that show the org's cut on every item** | Fundraise section, store page banner |
| Template-driven designs | **Original art for each org** | "Original designs" card |

## What not to do
- **Don't compete for Greek orders** until you're licensed (see below). Printing letters or crests without a license is the fastest way to get shut down.
- **Don't promise faster turnaround than your printer can deliver.** Set `turnaroundBusinessDays` in `data/site.json` from a real quote.
- **Don't name them on the site.** "The big guys" makes the point and keeps you out of trouble.

## Licensing, the real moat
- **Greek letters and crests** are licensed by each national organization, mostly through Affinity Consultants.
- **University marks** (name, logos, mascot) are licensed by the school, usually through CLC/Learfield. Check Miami University's licensing office.
- Non-Greek orgs using their **own name and original art** generally don't need a license. Club sports teams that use the university name are a gray area. Ask the licensing office before printing "Miami ___."
